"Good enough" gets a bad reputation. It sounds like a shrug. In delivery, it's the opposite: it's the only way to stop a project eating itself.

## Define it early

Before the build starts, write down what "good enough" means for this release — for the users who will live with it, not for the steering group that wants one more polish pass.

## Hold it under pressure

Scope pressure arrives dressed as quality. Extra edge cases. Extra integrations. Extra brand reviews. Each one is reasonable in isolation. Together they destroy the value of shipping.

## Kill the infinite revision cycle

If you can't say what would make you stop revising, you don't have a strategy. You have a mood.
