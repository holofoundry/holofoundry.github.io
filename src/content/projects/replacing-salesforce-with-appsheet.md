Salesforce is a brilliant product. Genuinely. For the right organisation, at the right scale, with the right budget and the right development team behind it, it's transformative.

The Trading Standards team at the London Borough of Hackney wasn't afforded those ideal conditions.

## The real problem

Officers needed case management they could trust in the field: create and track cases, allocate work, log inspections on a tablet, and bring Citizens Advice notifications into the same place — without a six-figure platform tax or a specialist Salesforce estate.

## What we built

The replacement was built on **AppSheet**, backed by Google Sheets as the data source. Before anyone raises an eyebrow at Google Sheets: it's the right call at this scale. The data volumes here don't need a relational database. They need auditability, accessibility, and zero infrastructure overhead for the council's IT team.

The platform does four things well:

1. **Case creation and management** — status, case type, assigned officer, linked premises, in one place.
2. **Case allocation** — supervisors balance workloads and see what's live across the team.
3. **Inspection logging in the field** — officers pull up a case on a tablet, conduct the inspection, and log findings on-site.
4. **Citizens Advice notification import** — complaint and referral intelligence that used to arrive as awkward XML/FTP hops.

## Why it worked

We solved the job officers actually do, not the enterprise CRM fantasy. The stack is boring on purpose. The outcome isn't: a tool people open every day.
